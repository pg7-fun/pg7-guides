Poker Hand Rankings: The Complete Reference [2026]

📅 Published April 9, 2026📝 8 min read👤 PG7 Team
PokerTexas Hold'emHand RankingsReference

This guide covers all standard five-card poker hands used in Texas Hold'em, Omaha, Seven Card Stud, and most other poker variants. Every hand is ranked from strongest to weakest.

Complete Hand Rankings

RankHand NameRequirementNotes
1Royal FlushA-K-Q-J-10 of the same suitThe highest possible hand. A, K, Q, J, and 10, all in the same suit.
2Straight FlushFive consecutive cards of the same suitFive sequential cards all in one suit. The highest straight flush wins.
3Four of a KindFour cards of the same rankFour cards with the same face value, e.g., four Kings.
4Full HouseThree of a kind plus a pairThree cards of one rank and two cards of another rank.
5FlushFive cards of the same suitAny five cards of the same suit that do not form a straight.
6StraightFive consecutive cards of mixed suitsFive cards in sequential order but of different suits.
7Three of a KindThree cards of the same rankThree cards with the same face value.
8Two PairTwo different pairsTwo separate pairs of cards with different ranks.
9One PairTwo cards of the same rankTwo cards that share the same rank.
10High CardNo matching combinationWhen no player has any of the above combinations, the highest card wins.

Tie-Breaking Rules

When two players hold the same hand type, use these rules: compare the highest-ranking card(s) within the hand, then the kicker (the next highest unpaired card), then subsequent kickers until a winner is found.

Probability Reference

Five-Card Draw Probability

In a standard 52-card deck, the approximate odds of making each hand are:

  • Royal Flush: 1 in 649,740
  • Straight Flush: 1 in 72,193
  • Four of a Kind: 1 in 4,165
  • Full House: 1 in 694
  • Flush: 1 in 509
  • Straight: 1 in 255
  • Three of a Kind: 1 in 47
  • Two Pair: 1 in 21
  • One Pair: 1 in 2.4
  • High Card: roughly 1 in 2
